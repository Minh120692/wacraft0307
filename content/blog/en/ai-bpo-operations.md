Outsourcing operations is usually framed as adding people. In practice the result is decided by
**how the work is divided** before anyone is hired. At WA+CRAFT we separate what AI can process from
what a person must judge, and only then design the team around it.

## 1. Split the work into three layers

- **Routine** — product registration, data cleanup, report aggregation. Automated with AI and scripts.
- **Semi-routine** — social post drafts, article outlines, rough video cuts. AI writes the first pass, a person finishes it.
- **Judgment** — positioning, pricing, exception handling with customers. Owned by people.

Automating before those layers are written down tends to multiply exceptions, and exceptions are what
actually cost money.

## 2. How we decide what to automate

A task is a good automation candidate when all three hold:

1. The input format is stable.
2. Failures are detectable.
3. Redoing the work is cheap.

Starting with tasks that satisfy all three gets results within weeks rather than quarters.

## 3. Put it into operation

Every automated step ships with measurement: volume processed, error rate, and how often a person had
to step in. Reviewed weekly, those three numbers show where the next investment belongs.

> Automation is not a one-off rollout. It is the ongoing work of redrawing the boundary.
